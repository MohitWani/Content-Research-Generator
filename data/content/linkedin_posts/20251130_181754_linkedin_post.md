Your LLM just failed a simple math problem. Here's the prompting technique that boosted accuracy by 74%.

Chain of Thought (CoT) prompting changed everything about how I build AI systems.

The concept is beautifully simple: instead of asking for direct answers, you prompt the model to "show its work" - just like we learned in school.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗶𝗳𝗳𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗶𝘀 𝗱𝗿𝗮𝗺𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗰:

❌ Traditional: "Roger has 5 balls, buys 2 cans of 3 balls each. Total?"
Result: Often wrong

✅ CoT: Same question + "Let's think step by step"
Result: "Roger started with 5. Two cans × 3 balls = 6 balls. 5 + 6 = 11."

That simple phrase - "Let's think step by step" - is Zero-Shot CoT, and it works across models without any examples.

𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗺𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝘀𝘆𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗺𝘀:

→ Complex business logic becomes explainable
→ Debugging is actually possible (you see the reasoning)
→ Accuracy improves on multi-step workflows
→ Stakeholders can audit AI decisions

I've deployed this in financial analysis, customer support routing, and data validation pipelines. The transparency alone justifies the extra tokens.

𝗣𝗿𝗼 𝘁𝗶𝗽: Combine CoT with self-consistency - generate multiple reasoning paths and pick the most common answer. It's like ensemble methods for prompting.

The Google Research paper (Wei et al., 2022) is worth reading if you're building anything that requires reasoning under the hood.
