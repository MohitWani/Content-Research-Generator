When the architect of the scaling hypothesis declares it's reached its limits, the field needs to listen.

Ilya Sutskever—co-inventor of AlexNet, former OpenAI Chief Scientist, the mind behind GPT-1 through GPT-4—has made a remarkable intellectual pivot. His message: the paradigm that dominated 2020-2025 is fundamentally constrained.

The scaling era gave us empirical power laws. We learned that L = (N_c / N)^α_N + (D_c / D)^α_D + L_∞ held with surprising consistency. We rode the exponential: 1.5B → 175B → 1.7T parameters. We built $500M+ training runs.

But Sutskever now articulates what many researchers are experiencing: we're approaching the asymptote. Not because we lack compute or data, but because the fundamental mechanism—next-token prediction on internet-scale corpora—has intrinsic limitations.

His $3B Safe Superintelligence venture isn't just another lab. It's a technical thesis: AGI requires algorithmic innovations beyond scale.

What does post-scaling AI look like?

→ Moving from passive prediction to active reasoning
→ Synthetic data generation with formal verification
→ Systems that can validate their own outputs
→ Architectures optimized for inference-time compute, not just parameter count

The transition from Age of Scaling to what Sutskever calls the "Age of Wonder and Discovery" mirrors the shift from AlexNet to Transformers—not incremental improvement, but fundamental reconceptualization.

For those of us working on frontier models, this isn't pessimism. It's clarity. The question isn't whether to scale, but what to scale and how to measure progress when perplexity stops correlating with capability.

The researchers who internalize this shift early will define the next era.
