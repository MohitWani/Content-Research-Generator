Your AI model works great in development. Then production hits and everything falls apart.

I've seen this pattern dozens of times. Here's what practitioners often miss when deploying AI systems:

𝗠𝗼𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗼𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 ≠ 𝗟𝗼𝗴𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗴
You need dedicated ML observability. Track:
• Input data drift (distribution shifts)
• Prediction confidence scores
• Latency at p95/p99
• Model staleness metrics

Tools like Evidently AI or Arize make this production-ready in hours, not weeks.

𝗩𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗘𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴
Not just your model weights. Version:
• Training data snapshots
• Feature engineering code
• Preprocessing pipelines
• Hyperparameters

MLflow or DVC integrated into your CI/CD pipeline saves you when (not if) you need to roll back.

𝗙𝗮𝗹𝗹𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗦𝘁𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗴𝗶𝗲𝘀
Always have a plan B:
• Rule-based backup system
• Cached predictions for common inputs
• Graceful degradation paths
• Circuit breakers for model timeouts

Your users shouldn't see a 500 error because your model endpoint is slow.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗸 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁𝘀 𝗔𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝗗𝗲𝗽𝗹𝗼𝘆𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁
Set up automated retraining pipelines. Data drift is inevitable. Your model from 6 months ago is probably underperforming right now.

The best AI practitioners I know spend 30% of their time on the model and 70% on the infrastructure around it.
