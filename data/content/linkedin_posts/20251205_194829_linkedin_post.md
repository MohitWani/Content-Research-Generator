Your AI model works great in testing, but falls apart in production. Here's why—and how to fix it.

After deploying dozens of ML systems, I've learned that the gap between prototype and production isn't technical—it's operational.

Here are 3 critical practices that separate hobby projects from production AI:

𝟭. 𝗠𝗼𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗼𝗿 𝗗𝗮𝘁𝗮 𝗗𝗿𝗶𝗳𝘁, 𝗡𝗼𝘁 𝗝𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗠𝗼𝗱𝗲𝗹 𝗠𝗲𝘁𝗿𝗶𝗰𝘀

Your F1 score means nothing when input distributions shift. Implement statistical tests (KS test, PSI) to catch drift early.

Quick win: Log feature statistics at inference time and compare to training baselines weekly.

𝟮. 𝗕𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱 𝗙𝗮𝗹𝗹𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗟𝗼𝗴𝗶𝗰 𝗙𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝗗𝗮𝘆 𝗢𝗻𝗲

AI should enhance your system, not be a single point of failure. Always have a rule-based or simpler model fallback.

Pattern I use:
- Primary: Complex ML model
- Fallback: Simple heuristic
- Circuit breaker: Confidence thresholds

𝟯. 𝗧𝗿𝗲𝗮𝘁 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗺𝗽𝘁𝘀 𝗮𝘀 𝗖𝗼𝗱𝗲

For LLM applications, version control your prompts. Use templating systems. Test systematically.

I've seen teams lose weeks of optimization because prompts lived in Slack threads instead of Git repos.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝗼𝘁𝘁𝗼𝗺 𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗲:

Production AI isn't about the fanciest model. It's about reliability, observability, and graceful degradation.

Build boring infrastructure around exciting models.
