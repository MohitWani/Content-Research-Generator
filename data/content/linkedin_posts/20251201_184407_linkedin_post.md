Your AI model works in testing. Then production hits and everything falls apart. Here's why—and how to fix it.

After deploying 20+ ML systems, I've learned that the gap between prototype and production isn't technical—it's operational.

Here are 5 production lessons that saved my team countless hours:

𝟭. 𝗠𝗼𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗼𝗿 𝗱𝗮𝘁𝗮 𝗱𝗿𝗶𝗳𝘁, 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝗷𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗺𝗼𝗱𝗲𝗹 𝗺𝗲𝘁𝗿𝗶𝗰𝘀
Your accuracy might be stable, but if input distributions shift, you're flying blind. Implement statistical tests (KS, PSI) on features, not just outputs.

𝟮. 𝗕𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱 𝗳𝗮𝗹𝗹𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝗰 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝗱𝗮𝘆 𝗼𝗻𝗲
AI fails. Always. Have rule-based systems or simpler models ready to take over. Your users won't care about your neural network when it's down.

𝟯. 𝗟𝗼𝗴 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 (𝗯𝘂𝘁 𝘀𝗺𝗮𝗿𝘁𝗹𝘆)
Capture input features, predictions, confidence scores, and timestamps. But use sampling strategies—you don't need 100% of high-confidence predictions logged.

𝟰. 𝗩𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗱𝗮𝘁𝗮, 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝗷𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗰𝗼𝗱𝗲
DVC, MLflow, or even simple manifest files. You need to reproduce any model by knowing exactly what data trained it.

𝟱. 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝗮 𝘀𝗵𝗮𝗱𝗼𝘄 𝗺𝗼𝗱𝗲
Run your new model alongside the old one in production without affecting users. Compare results. Catch issues before they impact business metrics.

The best AI systems aren't the ones with the highest accuracy—they're the ones that stay reliable at 3 AM when nobody's watching.
