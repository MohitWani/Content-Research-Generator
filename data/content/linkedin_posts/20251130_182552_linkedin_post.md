Your AI model works great in the lab. Then production hits and everything falls apart.

Here's what 3 years of deploying ML systems taught me about the gap between prototype and production:

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗖𝗵𝗮𝗹𝗹𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗲𝘀 𝗡𝗼 𝗢𝗻𝗲 𝗧𝗮𝗹𝗸𝘀 𝗔𝗯𝗼𝘂𝘁:

→ Data drift isn't theoretical. Your model trained on last quarter's data is making decisions on completely different patterns today.

→ Latency matters more than accuracy. A 95% accurate model that takes 5 seconds is useless when users expect sub-second responses.

→ Monitoring is not optional. You need to track prediction distributions, feature drift, and business metrics—not just model accuracy.

→ Rollback strategies save careers. Always have a way to quickly revert to the previous model when things go sideways at 2 AM.

𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗔𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀:

✓ Start with simple baselines. That complex transformer might be overkill when a well-tuned gradient boosting model does the job.

✓ Build observability from day one. Instrument everything—inference time, input distributions, prediction confidence.

✓ Implement shadow mode first. Run your new model alongside the old one without affecting users. Compare outputs, catch issues early.

✓ Version everything. Models, data, feature engineering code, and preprocessing pipelines. Your future self will thank you.

The best AI practitioners I know spend 20% of their time on models and 80% on infrastructure, monitoring, and operational excellence.

Because a model that works reliably at scale beats a slightly better model that's fragile every single time.
